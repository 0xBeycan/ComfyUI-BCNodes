Write one English text-to-image prompt that rebuilds the scene of this image: the same place, the same people, the same moment. Reply with the prompt alone, as one flowing paragraph of plain, everyday English, at most 300 words.

Write the paragraph in this order.

1. The opening sentence, made of these parts alone: `A` or `An` [camera angle] [genre] [framing] [`selfie`, `mirror selfie`, `POV shot` or `shot`] `of` [what the scene is of].
- Camera angle: eye-level, high-angle, low-angle or overhead.
- Genre: the kind of photograph in one or two words, such as candid, street, editorial or amateur.
- Framing, from the lowest part of the body in the scene: extreme close-up when the eyes or mouth are cut off, close-up for the chin to the collarbones, chest-up for the chest, half-body for the waist, hips or thighs, three-quarter for the knees, full-body for the feet.
- Each person is `a` or `an` [age]`-year-old` [ethnicity] `woman` or `man`, the ethnicity named by a people or a region, never by a colour. Take every person as 18 or over and describe them so.
- A scene of a body part is `the` [body part] `of` the person.
- A place the image makes certain takes `in` and its proper name.

2. Each person, in this order: what they are, what they wear, what they do.
- What they are: their physical features on the side that faces the camera, as far as the scene holds them: the colour, length, texture and cut of their hair, face shape, nose, eyes, brows, lips and build; each mole, freckle and mark on their skin, however small; the makeup and nail polish the image makes certain.
- What they wear: each garment by the exact name of its cut, with its fabric, colour and how tight it is, and how it sits on the body. A see-through garment is `sheer` with its noun and its fabric, and a garment worn under it is told as a garment of its own.
- What they do: the action by its plain verb, the hands, the gaze and the expression, and how far the body is turned: facing the camera, at three-quarters, in profile, at three-quarters from behind, or with the back to the camera. Give the head's turn only where it differs from the body's.
- After the opening sentence, call a person she, he, her or his. Where that could mean two people, call them by their [age]-year-old [ethnicity] woman or man from the opening sentence.

3. The rest of the scene, in the order fluent English gives it. A light that comes from or through a thing, such as a lamp, a window or a screen, follows that thing directly.

4. One sentence on the light from outside the scene, with that light as its subject and a source that is neither a person nor a thing in the scene: what light it is by its source, how bright, soft and coloured it is, where it comes from as the camera sees it, and what it falls on, with the light and shadow it leaves there.

5. One last sentence on an effect the camera lays over the whole scene, such as film grain, flash glare or a colour cast.

Throughout:
- Tell the scene at one instant, each thing by what it is and what it does, as a fact of that thing: a lamp stands on the desk, she holds a cup. Every sentence is about the scene itself, never about the picture, its frame or someone looking at it.
- Tell only what is there, each fact once, each action by its plain, direct verb.
- Place each thing by a person, by another thing or by the camera. Left and right of a body part are the person's own; left and right of anything else are the camera's.
- Of a thing only partly in the scene, tell the part that is there.
- A thing not worn that covers part of a person is the subject of its phrase, as in a blanket covers her legs, and the part it covers takes its name alone.
- A thing in a mirror is reflected in it; a thing on a screen is on it.
- Name the brand of a thing when the image makes it certain. Tell a logo by its shape, colour, place and brand, with its lettering in English double quotes where the brand name does not already hold those words.
- Copy text in the scene letter for letter, in its own language, inside English double quotes, as text on its thing: a sign reads "OPEN". The text keeps its own words even where they break a rule here.
- A quality or a blur belongs in the phrase of the thing it describes.
- A watermark, caption or other mark laid over the image stays out of the prompt, and so does the proper name of a place the image does not make certain.
